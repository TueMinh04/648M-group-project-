3. Hospital Clinical Guidelines & Competency Checklists

Virtucare’s simulation curriculum is directly mapped to accredited hospital clinical standards from Scarborough Health Network and Humber River Health, addressing critical procedural errors, mandatory sterile techniques, and placement verification rules across three foundational clinical procedures.

Clinical Standard: Scarborough Health Network (Practice Standard SHN-PRSTAND-0102)
Hospital Accredited
Common Training Gap: Tube inadvertently inserted into the lung / bronchial airway.

Adult Critical Steps

Check doctor’s order & confirm 2 patient identifiers
Collect equipment and verify contraindications (e.g. base of skull fracture)
Measure length of tube to insert (NEX: Nose to Ear lobe to Xiphoid process)
Lubricate tube with water-soluble jelly
Insert tube into nare while instructing patient to swallow water through a straw
Secure tube and document insertion depth at nare
Adult Placement Verification:
Chest X-ray initially prior to any feed or medication; measure external tube length every 4 hours.

Neonate Critical Steps

Check doctor’s order and verify infant identity
Collect neonatal-specific soft feeding tube equipment
Accurately measure insertion depth (Nose to Ear to Mid-sternum/Xiphoid)
Lubricate tube with sterile water (never petroleum or heavy oils)
Insert tube smoothly and quickly into nare
Provide supportive pain management / non-nutritive sucking
Document tube size, length marker, and infant tolerance
Neonate Placement Verification:
Aspirate stomach contents and verify pH using pH test strip; pH < 5.5 confirms gastric placement.
Clinical Standard: Humber River Health (Reference No. 2734)
Hospital Accredited
Common Training Gap: Inability to locate the urethra (in female patients); breaking sterility of sterile field; inflating balloon inside urethra.

Critical Clinical Steps

Check doctor’s order & clinical indication for catheterization
Collect sterile catheter kit, gloves, and correct French size
Maintain absolute strict sterile technique throughout setup and insertion
Lubricate catheter tip with sterile lidocaine/water-soluble jelly
Insert tube gently and advance until clear urine flow is observed
Advance further to bifurcation before attaching syringe to inflate balloon with sterile water
Do not exceed three attempts per patient encounter
Document catheter type, size, balloon volume, and urine characteristics
Must be clinically re-assessed every 24 hours to minimize CAUTI risk
Placement Verification:
Direct visualization of urine flow through catheter tubing into drainage bag; gentle pull back to resistance once balloon is inflated.
Clinical Standard: Humber River Health (Vascular Access Devices Policy)
Hospital Accredited
Common Training Gap: Selecting wrong catheter gauge; missing vein on initial puncture; undetected interstitial extravasation.

Critical Clinical Steps

Check doctor’s order, verify identity, and assess clinical need
Select appropriate cannula gauge (e.g. 20G/22G for routine infusions)
Apply tourniquet to engorge distal veins (start distal, save proximal veins)
Disinfect site with 2% chlorhexidine in 70% alcohol and let air dry completely
Anchor vein, insert needle bevel up at 15–30 degree angle until blood flashback
Lower angle, advance plastic cannula over needle into vein, and release tourniquet
Flush with sterile 0.9% saline to confirm free-flowing patency without swelling
Secure cannula with sterile transparent Tegaderm dressing
Limit to no more than 2 attempts per clinician
Assess site every hour during continuous infusions for signs of phlebitis or infiltration
Placement Verification:
Immediate visualization of blood flashback in flash chamber; smooth saline flush with zero resistance, pain, or blanching tissue swelling.
