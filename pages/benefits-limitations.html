2. Our Proposal: Proposed Improvements, Benefits & Limitations

Our group’s proposed improvements to clinical procedural training: contrasting the long-term simulation architecture with current prototype boundaries and evidence-based benefits.

Technology Overview

Concept prototype. Not clinically validated.

What it is: A concept for an AI-assisted simulation platform where students practice clinical procedures before treating real patients.

Vision vs. prototype: The long-term vision is VR modules built from consented, anonymized recordings, and what we built is a 2D browser prototype with rule-based feedback for three procedures.

Implementation scope: Developed strictly as an academic concept demo using deterministic JavaScript logic for ethical analysis—not an autonomous or clinically validated medical device.

Benefits & Limitations

A balanced, honest assessment of educational utility versus current prototype boundaries:

Balanced Academic Assessment

Benefits

  • Safe, repeatable practice before real patients. Simulation training is linked to better knowledge and skills, with moderate effects on patient outcomes. [ahrq]
  • Skill-based progression instead of elapsed training hours.
  • Feedback after each attempt. Feedback is moderately effective for procedural skills. [elsevierpure]
  • Steps checked against hospital standards, and an accessible 2D mode that needs no headset.

Limitations

  • Transfer to real patients is unproven for these procedures. Transfer has been shown for surgical and endoscopic procedures. [adelaide]
  • The prototype is 2D and scripted, with no validated feedback.
  • Anonymization lowers re-identification risk but does not remove it. One study estimated 99.98% of Americans could be re-identified from 15 demographic attributes. [uclouvain]
  • Learners may over-trust automated feedback. Users often miss new errors that decision support introduces. [oup]
  • Unequal access: VR headsets, hardware devices and high-speed internet cost money.
  • Source recordings from few patients could bias the content (our inference).
Full analysis of risks and safeguards is detailed in the Ethical and Social Analysis section.

Our Proposal: Core Platform Improvements

To overcome the shortcomings of conventional static manikins and arbitrary seat-time training, our group proposes four foundational technological and pedagogical improvements:

Immersive VR scenarios

Proposed realistic procedural environments designed to place learners directly into simulated clinical settings, paired with an accessible 2D screen mode to prevent cognitive overload.

Hands-on clinical practice

Interactive procedural practice allowing trainees to build sequence memory, master checklist techniques, and refine decision-making in a risk-free environment through competency-based progression.

Performance tracking

Proposed objective metrics and kinematic motion tracking to compare trainee performance against expert baselines, delivering terminal post-task feedback for superior retention.

Standardized curriculum

Delivers identical, guideline-adherent training modules across all learners, eliminating clinical variability in student opportunities.

Comparative Analysis

A direct comparison between traditional clinical training shortcomings and how our group's proposal transforms procedural education:

How skills are taught today

  • Clinicians learn skills only when rare live patient opportunities arise.

  • Independent practice is restricted until clinical judgement is proven.

  • This means a clinician’s first attempt at a critical skill often occurs on a live patient.

How Virtucare teaches the skill

  • Hands-on repetitive practice without live patient risk.

  • Objective performance feedback with non-disruptive terminal debriefing.

  • Accessible anytime-anywhere learning environment to master complex procedures.
