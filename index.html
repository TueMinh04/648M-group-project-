AI-Assisted Clinical Simulation

A conceptual proposal for using AI to transform clinical procedure training into safe, scalable, and personalized learning experiences for healthcare students.

1. The Problem

Clinical procedural training today relies primarily on physical simulation labs and static manikin-based practice. While foundational, these approaches carry significant structural limitations:

Shortcomings of Existing Technology

  • Lack of Realism: Traditional VR requires manual 3D modeling by artists, which is incredibly expensive and fails to capture the true, deformative nature of human tissue.

  • Limited Scenarios: Rare or highly complex surgical procedures are practically impossible to simulate due to the cost of bespoke 3D modeling.

  • Subjective Assessment: Feedback on surgical technique relies heavily on the physical presence of an expert instructor, creating a bottleneck for large-scale practice.

Why This Matters

Surgical confidence and patient safety are directly linked. When residents lack sufficient high-fidelity practice opportunities — especially for complex procedures — operative times increase and clinical errors become more likely.

There is a clear need for a training approach that can automatically generate true-to-life interactive modules directly from real-world surgical recordings.

2. Our Proposal: CLINOVA

We propose CLINOVA — an AI-powered pipeline that transforms real clinical video into interactive, web-based 2D practice modules, providing automated kinematic feedback to the learner.

AI Video Processing

Using computer vision, our AI pipeline processes real, consented clinical video and extracts procedural steps and patient anatomy to generate interactive 2D simulations.

Interactive Web Practice

Students access a simulated web environment, using interactive sliders and UI controls to manipulate medical instruments and simulate clinical tasks safely.

Kinematic AI Feedback

The AI tracks the student's inputs (such as insertion speed and applied force), comparing their simulated performance against an expert baseline to provide objective feedback.

How CLINOVA Differs

Current VR Training

  • Requires expensive VR headsets

  • Modules take months to manually build

  • Looks like a cartoon video game

  • No real-time objective motion tracking

CLINOVA Proposal

  • Accessible directly in any web browser

  • Modules generated in hours via AI

  • Interactive 2D web simulations

  • Simulated kinematic force tracking

4. Ethical Analysis

Using AI in clinical education raises important ethical and social questions. Our proposal addresses each of these directly.

Consent

Concern: Patients and learners may not fully understand how recordings or session data are used in an AI training context.

Approach: All data collection uses layered, plain-language informed consent. Consent can be withdrawn at any point without academic penalty.

Fairness & Bias

Concern: AI training scenarios may reflect biased clinical data — under-representing certain demographics or rare conditions.

Approach: Mandatory diversity audits of all source materials. Active recruitment of varied clinical scenarios across institutions.

Privacy

Concern: Learner performance data could be misused for high-stakes evaluations or shared without consent.

Approach: Clear purpose limitation policies. Learners retain ownership of their performance data and can request deletion.

Safety & Accuracy

Concern: AI feedback may be incorrect, reinforcing wrong clinical behaviors if not reviewed.

Approach: "Human-in-the-loop" policy — a clinician educator can flag, override, or correct any AI feedback at any time.

Accessibility & Equity

Concern: Browser-based tools still require reliable internet and devices, which not all learners have equally.

Approach: Designed to run in lightweight 2D mode on low-spec devices. Offline-first caching is planned for future iterations.

Role of the Educator

Concern: AI feedback tools may be perceived as reducing the need for human clinical instructors.

Approach: CLINOVA is a supplement, not a replacement. Educator oversight is built into the review layer of every session.
