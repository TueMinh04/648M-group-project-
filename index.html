AI-Assisted Clinical Simulation

A conceptual proposal for using AI to transform clinical procedure training into safe, scalable, and personalized learning experiences for healthcare students.

Team: Youngin Choi, Yvonne Ma, Paul Leung, Minh Vuong

1. The Problem

Clinical procedural training today relies primarily on physical simulation labs and manikin-based practice. While foundational, these approaches carry significant structural limitations:

Shortcomings of Existing Technology

  • Limited Availability: Simulation labs require scheduled access, trained staff, and expensive equipment — not equally accessible to all institutions.

  • No Adaptive Feedback: Manikins and physical models cannot tailor difficulty or feedback to individual learner performance in real time.

  • Scalability Ceiling: Each session requires physical setup and direct supervision, making large-scale or self-directed practice impractical.

  • Procedural Gaps: Rare or high-risk procedures are infrequently practiced due to limited safe exposure opportunities.

Why This Matters

Procedural confidence directly affects patient safety. When learners lack sufficient practice opportunities — especially outside structured lab hours — clinical errors become more likely.

There is a clear need for a training approach that is accessible on-demand, adaptive to the learner, and safe to repeat without resource constraints.

2. Our Proposal: VIRTUCARE

We propose VIRTUCARE — a web-based, AI-assisted clinical simulation platform. Rather than replacing physical labs, VIRTUCARE supplements them by providing structured, consent-governed, AI-guided practice sessions accessible anytime, from any browser.

Consent-First Design

Every session begins with an explicit, layered consent process. Learners understand what data is collected, how AI is used, and how to withdraw at any point.

Guided Procedural Practice

Learners work through step-by-step clinical scenarios (e.g., NGT insertion, IV placement) in an interactive, decision-based format — repeatable on demand.

AI-Generated Feedback

AI analyzes learner decisions and generates structured feedback on accuracy, sequencing, and safety. A human clinician can review or override feedback at any time.

How VIRTUCARE Differs

Current Approach

  • Scheduled lab time required

  • Fixed, non-adaptive scenarios

  • Expert supervisor must be physically present

  • No persistent learner data across sessions

VIRTUCARE Proposal

  • On-demand, browser-based access

  • Adaptive difficulty based on performance

  • AI feedback with clinician oversight layer

  • Optional longitudinal progress tracking

Self-paced. Decision-based. Repeatable.

Upon completion, AI evaluates the session. Feedback is structured around accuracy, safety sequencing, and missed steps — phrased constructively, not as a binary pass/fail grade.

Reflective. Iterative. Learner-controlled.

4. Ethical Analysis

Using AI in clinical education raises important ethical and social questions. Our proposal addresses each of these directly.

Consent

Concern: Patients and learners may not fully understand how recordings or session data are used in an AI training context.

Approach: All data collection uses layered, plain-language informed consent. Consent can be withdrawn at any point without academic penalty.

Fairness & Bias

Concern: AI training scenarios may reflect biased clinical data — under-representing certain demographics or rare conditions.

Approach: Mandatory diversity audits of all source materials. Active recruitment of varied clinical scenarios across institutions.

Privacy

Concern: Learner performance data could be misused for high-stakes evaluations or shared without consent.

Approach: Clear purpose limitation policies. Learners retain ownership of their performance data and can request deletion.

Safety & Accuracy

Concern: AI feedback may be incorrect, reinforcing wrong clinical behaviors if not reviewed.

Approach: "Human-in-the-loop" policy — a clinician educator can flag, override, or correct any AI feedback at any time.

Accessibility & Equity

Concern: Browser-based tools still require reliable internet and devices, which not all learners have equally.

Approach: Designed to run in lightweight 2D mode on low-spec devices. Offline-first caching is planned for future iterations.

Role of the Educator

Concern: AI feedback tools may be perceived as reducing the need for human clinical instructors.

Approach: VIRTUCARE is a supplement, not a replacement. Educator oversight is built into the review layer of every session.
